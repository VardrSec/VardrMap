# v0.42.0 — stop-work pauses schedules

**Date:** 2026-10-10

**Behaviour change.** Stop-work now stops a stopped engagement's **schedules**, not just the claim
of the jobs they produce. No migration.

## What was wrong

Stop-work refused the claim (`403 stop_work_active`) but did nothing about schedules.
`_materialize_due_schedules` ran on every runner poll with no stop-work check, so while an
engagement was stopped each schedule kept queueing one pending job per interval behind the
refusal. Measured against the real code: six hourly intervals under stop-work left **six pending
jobs**, and all six became claimable the moment stop-work was released — a burst of repeated
scans at the instant work resumed. For `ffuf` and `dalfox` that is repeated *active* traffic.

It was pre-existing for every tool and was documented, then pinned by a test, in v0.40.0 so that
any change would be conscious. This is that change.

## What it does now

- While stop-work is engaged, a stopped engagement's due schedules are **skipped**: no job is
  queued.
- `next_run_at` is **not advanced** and the schedule stays enabled. On release each schedule fires
  **exactly one** catch-up job on the next poll — the same rule as after a runner outage ("one
  catch-up job, not one per missed interval") — and then resumes its normal cadence.
- Other engagements' schedules are unaffected.
- Claim-time refusal is unchanged, so a job that was already pending when stop-work was engaged
  is still refused.

The alternative was to advance `next_run_at` while skipping, so nothing fires on release. Firing
once was chosen for consistency with the outage rule and because releasing stop-work is itself a
deliberate act; an operator who does not want that disables the schedule.

## Tests

The test that pinned the old behaviour is replaced by four: nothing queues across four intervals
(for both `ffuf` and `httpx`, since the fix is tool-agnostic), the schedule is left untouched and
fires once on release with no second job inside the interval, and another engagement's schedule
keeps running. Removing the fix turns all four red.

893 backend tests.
